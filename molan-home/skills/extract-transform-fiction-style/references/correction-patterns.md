# Generalizable Correction Patterns

These patterns are reusable diagnostics derived from writing revisions. They are not universal bans. Activate them only when supported by the user's preferences or selected de-AI strength.

## Extraction Rule

For every correction, capture four layers:

1. Surface edit: what changed in wording or order.
2. Functional problem: what failed in the scene.
3. Reusable principle: the smallest rule that transfers.
4. Boundary: where the old form may still be valid.

Do not retain project names, plot outcomes, faction rules, unique items, or cultivation terminology in a portable correction library.

## Logic And Evidence

### Unsupported history

Problem: narration invents a character's past habits solely to manufacture contrast.

Replacement: establish past behavior earlier, derive it from supplied canon, or show only the present character's observable surprise.

### Evidence contamination

Problem: an allegedly competent, evidence-aware character casually rubs, moves, destroys, or exposes a clue without necessity.

Replacement: preserve the evidence, use an appropriate tool, or make contamination an intentional choice with a cost.

### Conclusion outruns evidence

Problem: one visual clue proves source, duration, culprit, motive, and method at once.

Replacement: distinguish observation, suspicion, working hypothesis, confirmation, and proof. Let later evidence upgrade confidence.

### Unsupported dominance

Problem: a weaker character changes the power relationship with a forceful line but no rule, evidence, leverage, environment, identity, or ally.

Replacement: let the character stall, bargain, retreat, pay a cost, or use a previously established lever.

### Duplicate opponent scheme

Problem: the opponent has already gained the desired benefit, then takes an extra high-risk action that achieves the same benefit only to create another twist.

Replacement: give each action a distinct gain or loss avoided. Remove any action whose incentive cannot be stated clearly.

## Formulaic Reasoning

### Negative parallel explanation

Pattern: `不是A，而是B。做C，再做D，比E更好。`

Problem: thought becomes a polished essay with a complete thesis and symmetrical logic.

Replacement: allow omission, inversion, interruption, sensory intrusion, or a partial inference appropriate to the moment. Keep the full structure only when the speaker is deliberately arguing or formally analyzing.

### Symmetrical branch calculation

Pattern: `若A则B；若C则D。`

Problem: exposes every branch of an opponent's calculation and removes uncertainty.

Replacement: reveal intent through a glance, delay, selective action, or one incomplete suspicion. Use full branch analysis only for a character whose defining behavior is explicit tactical calculation.

### Complete intention chain

Pattern: `本想A，看到B，又想到C，于是改做D。`

Problem: renders a fleeting thought as a complete causal report.

Replacement: omit obvious links. Let the abandoned action, a brief perception, or the final choice imply the change.

### Tutorial negotiation

Pattern: identify violation, state demand, hear rebuttal, concede one point, invoke neutral authority, win procedural advantage.

Problem: conflict follows a flawless complaint workflow with little emotion, interruption, status pressure, or risk.

Replacement: preserve the valid leverage but let speech reflect urgency, anger, pride, fear, relationship, and unequal power. Do not force irrationality; break the procedural symmetry.

## Exposition And Description

### Encyclopedic injection

Problem: one paragraph explains a process, resource allocation, penalties, exceptions, and historical background.

Replacement: introduce the immediate consequence first. Distribute only necessary rules through current action, dialogue, memory, or later complications.

### Urgency inversion

Problem: a character under a deadline answers secondary questions completely before stating the urgent goal.

Replacement: lead with the action or deadline. Defer explanations unless they alter the immediate choice.

### Static object inventory

Pattern: front inscription, damaged corner, back stain, material, color, and age listed in balanced order.

Problem: the object pauses the scene and reads like catalog metadata.

Replacement: reveal details as the character handles the object; select only details that affect identity, evidence, use, or emotion.

### Decorative environment

Problem: detail does not affect movement, mood, evidence, choice, or future action.

Replacement: remove it or connect it to a concrete scene function.

### Inaccurate metaphor

Problem: comparison sounds literary but fails spatial, sensory, or causal accuracy.

Replacement: use a direct verb or a comparison the viewpoint character could naturally make. A metaphor must sharpen perception, not merely decorate it.

## Character And Dialogue

### Flat dialogue

Problem: every character uses complete neutral sentences and differs only in stated opinion.

Replacement: vary particles, titles, omissions, sentence length, interruptions, favored words, and what each person refuses to say. Tie each line to a goal.

### Trait labels without enactment

Problem: narration declares someone clever, cold, kind, or villainous.

Replacement: show the trait through choices, gains sought, costs accepted, speech habits, selective attention, and contradictions under pressure.

### Body part as artificial subject

Problem: a pen, gaze, fingertip, or voice repeatedly acts independently when the person is the natural subject.

Replacement: write the person acting unless the isolated body detail creates a precise perceptual effect.

### Synchronized crowd response

Problem: everyone freezes, gasps, falls silent, or shares one emotion at the same instant.

Replacement: select one or two consequential reactions, or show divided interests in the group.

## Sentence And Paragraph Waste

### Meaningless contrast

Pattern: `X很纤细，却很稳` or similar `X却Y` construction where no real expectation is overturned.

Replacement: state the useful action directly or establish the expectation that makes the contrast meaningful.

### Mechanical capability explanation

Problem: narration says a body part still works after the character's action has already proved it.

Replacement: retain the action and any relevant pain, limitation, or cost; remove the explanatory label.

### Redundant transition

Problem: a sentence announces that characters met a deadline or left a location after the preceding actions already show it, without changing the scene.

Replacement: end on the functional action or move directly to the next consequential beat.

### Restated emotion

Problem: gesture communicates anger, concern, or fear, then narration labels the same emotion.

Replacement: trust the gesture or add a new mixed emotion, motive, or consequence.

### Generic climax line

Problem: narration predicts a storm, declares dead silence, or says only one sentence remained.

Replacement: end on a specific arrival, demand, discovery, injury, decision, deadline, or immediate consequence.

## Mechanics Clarity

Problem: power or bodily movement uses vague terms such as “压进去” without stating what enters where, how it travels, what resists, where it fails, and what happens.

Replacement checklist:

- identify the acting person and body part or tool;
- name the energy, force, or material;
- state entry point and path when relevant;
- show resistance or limitation;
- show failure point or successful effect;
- preserve injury, cost, and timing afterward.

Do not over-explain routine movement. Use this detail only when mechanics affect plausibility or outcome.

## Promotion Policy

- One correction creates a candidate rule.
- A correction plus explicit rationale usually creates a confirmed scoped rule.
- Repeated corrections to the same structure may create a hard rule.
- A later explicit exception narrows scope; it does not necessarily invalidate the rule.
- A project-fact correction never becomes a prose rule unless the user states a general principle behind it.
